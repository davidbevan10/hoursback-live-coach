Contract recruitment call

## Contract recruitment: the 20-minute call

Contract-led professional recruiters and groups integrating an acquisition (about 100-1,000 internal staff), plus public-sector temp and interim, managed service providers, and industrial and driver staffing with a central back office (150+). On the call: an operations or back-office leader, or the CEO, MD or CFO.

0-2 min: open2-15 min: questions15-20 min: close

## Before the call 2 minutes
 * They came from the Grip LinkedIn recruitment campaign. Check which one: REC-HOURS (COO, operations, integration, compliance, back-office titles) or REC-MONEY (CEO, MD, CFO, finance director). Lead with hours for the first group and with the candidate database for the second.
 * Check which message they replied to, and quote their reply back in the open.
 * The work we are listening for repeats every week for every contractor: timesheets, the payroll or umbrella handoff, compliance checks, invoicing and client reporting.
 * Triggers you may raise if they bring them up: umbrella company PAYE joint and several liability from 6 April 2026 (the agency is usually liable if an umbrella fails to pay), the Fair Work Agency launched 7 April 2026 (one enforcer for employment rights, including agency work), and any acquisition or new owner in the last six months.
 * Check internal (office) headcount, not temps on the books. Under 100 internal staff is outside this campaign.
 * Our recruitment figure (about 780 hours a year) is hours found by an assessment on the consultant desks. Nothing for that team has been built and measured yet.
 * Sequences pause 21 October to 4 November. If the call falls near that, check you can still send the page within two working days.

## What they were sent

Message 1, hours version (operations titles)

{first_name}, how much of a consultant's day at {company} goes on admin rather than calls?

Message 1, money version (CEO and finance titles)

{first_name}, how confident are you in the data in {company}'s candidate database?

Message 2

In one recruitment team we found about 780 hours a year across the team. The biggest items were on the consultant desks, including logging calls into the CRM by hand. We find where the hours go and what they're costing, then build the fixes. I've put it on one page if you'd like to see it?

## Open 0-2 minutes
 * "Thanks for making the time. You replied about [their reply], so I'd like to start there."
 * "Quickly on us: we interview people role by role, in confidence, show leadership where the hours go and what they cost, then build the fixes on the systems you already run. The hours go back to your existing team."
 * "Today I'd like 20 minutes to see where the hours go: the work that repeats every week for every contractor. At the end I'll tell you what I'd put on one page. Does that sound OK?"

## Five questions 2-15 minutes

## 1. The team

"Which team would we be looking at, and roughly how many people are in it? Is that one central back office, or spread across brands or offices?"

Sets who we would interview and the size of the piece. After an acquisition there may be two back offices doing the same job two ways.

Listen for: a named function (contractor care, payroll and billing, compliance, shared services) with a head count, and whether the work sits in one place or several.

## 2. The time map (must ask)

"Take one contractor through a normal week, from the timesheet to the invoice. Which steps does your team repeat, how often, and roughly how long does each one take?"

This is the table on the proposal. Get three to six tasks, each with how often and how long. Write it down and say it back: "So across the team that's about [X] hours a week, and the biggest items are [A] and [B]. Is that right?"

Listen for: chasing and checking timesheets, the payroll or umbrella handoff, right-to-work and compliance file checks, raising invoices, building client reports or MI by hand, typing the same details into more than one system. For the CEO or CFO: how far the candidate database can be trusted.

## 3. What it's worth (must ask)

"If your team got that time back, what would it go on, and what's that worth to you?"

Their own words go on the proposal. Write down what they say, not your sum.

Listen for: more contractors on the same team, a cleaner integration, compliance they can stand behind if an inspection comes, a candidate database a buyer would pay more for, or time back for the consultants to sell.

## 4. Who signs (must ask)

"Who approves spend like this, and what's their title? Is there anyone else who should be involved, say finance, compliance or whoever leads the integration?"

The page has to work for the person who signs, so it needs to know who that is.

Listen for: a name and title (COO, CFO, MD), whether the group or a PE owner has to agree, and anyone who would want to see it first.

## 5. Timing

"What else is going on over the next few months: an integration, a system change, year end, anything on umbrella or compliance?"

Tells us when interviews can happen and what the work has to fit around.

Listen for: a deal completing or systems being merged, a back-office platform change, year end, or a compliance review. A clear window is a good sign.

## Fits, and doesn't fit

## Signs it fits
 * A central back office runs timesheets, payroll or umbrella handoff, compliance and invoicing for contractors every week.
 * About 100-1,000 internal staff (contract-led), or 150+ (public-sector, MSP, industrial, driver).
 * An acquisition or new owner, with back offices to bring together.
 * Client reporting or MI built by hand each week or month.
 * A named operations, integration or finance leader who can commission the work.
 * They talk about growth, margin or getting more done with the team they have.

## Signs it doesn't
 * Health and social care staffing (nursing, locums, care), or anything NHS or GP.
 * Executive search, or a permanent-only firm under 100 internal staff.
 * An individual franchisee rather than the franchisor's central team.
 * Decisions sit with a global group outside the UK.
 * Recent news is mainly about job cuts.
 * They sell AI, automation, outsourcing or recruitment software of their own.

## If they say

"What about confidentiality and our data?"

We interview in confidence. Findings are shared with leadership at team level, so nothing identifies a person in what leadership sees.

"We hold right-to-work and vetting data. Do you need it?"

The interviews are about how the work gets done, so we don't need candidate or contractor records to map the week. Where a fix would touch that data, we set it out before anything is built.

"We have an IT team."

Good, we'd work with them. We build on the systems you already run, and the interviews show them which jobs are worth fixing first.

"We're mid-integration and too busy."

Understood. Tell me your window and I'll put the timing on the page around it.

"Have you done this for a contract back office?"

Not for a contractor back office yet. In one recruitment team we found about 780 hours a year across the team, mostly on the consultant desks. This call is how we find out whether your back office has repeated work like that.

"Just send me something."

Happy to. That's the one page I send after this call: what we'd look at, who we'd speak to, and the cost. A few more minutes now makes it about your team.

"What does it cost?"

"It depends on how many people we speak to; it'll be on the page."

## Close 15-20 minutes

I'll send you one page within two working days: what we'd look at, who we'd speak to, and the cost. Does that work?

## Before you hang up, have you got
 * The team or function, and roughly how many people are in it.
 * Three to six repeated tasks, each with how often and how long, said back and agreed.
 * Their own words on what the time would go on and what it's worth to them.
 * Who approves the spend and their title, plus anyone else who should be involved.
 * Timing: integration, system change, year end, or anything else going on.

## Never say
 * Any price on the call, the assessment price, any money-back guarantee, or "small business".
 * Any client name, system name, or a client's team size or pound figures.
 * "X hours going on [task]". Hours are whole-team totals; tasks are examples ("across the team; the biggest items were...").
 * "Saved". Say hours "found".
 * That anything has been built or delivered for the recruitment team behind the 780 figure.
 * 870 as anything but "an insurance broker".
 * Headcount cuts or replacing people. The hours go back to the existing team.
 * "Monitor", "track", "smallest task", "wasted wages", or anything that criticises their people.
 * That their data stays in the UK, or anything about where data is stored.
 * A view on whether they meet umbrella, IR35 or Fair Work Agency rules. That's for their advisers.
 * Figures, dates or rules you can't point to a source for.

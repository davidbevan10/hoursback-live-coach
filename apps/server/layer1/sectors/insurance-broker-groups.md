Broker groups call

## Broker groups: the 20-minute call

UK broker group, consolidator or large independent broker (about 150-2,000 staff). On the call: a COO, operations or integration leader (hours angle) or a CEO, MD or CFO (money angle).

0-2 min: open2-15 min: questions15-20 min: close

## Before the call 2 minutes
 * They came from the Grip LinkedIn campaign: connection with no note, then Message 1 chosen by job title (INS-HOURS for operations and integration titles, INS-MONEY for CEO, MD and finance titles), then Message 2 three days later. Check which one they replied to.
 * Message 2 offered "one page". Check whether you have already sent it, so you can refer to it.
 * Open their lead record: the trigger and its date (a firm bought, a new COO or integration director, new PE owner, brands merged, a plan to grow). That trigger is your way into question 5.
 * The pressure in this segment: groups are buying firms and bolting their back offices together, and each acquisition brings its own way of doing the same admin. There is usually a named person who owns integration.
 * The proof that fits them is Message 2: about 870 hours a year across one insurance broker's team. Whole-team figure; the tasks are examples.
 * If their recent news is mainly about job cuts, keep the tone low and talk about capacity for growth.
 * Sequences pause 21 October to 4 November. If the two working days for the page fall in that window, say the real date the page will arrive.

## What they were sent

Message 1, hours version (operations titles)

{first_name}, roughly how much of the week at {company} goes on typing the same details into more than one system?

Message 1, money version (CEO and finance titles)

{first_name}, does anyone at {company} have a figure for what the admin behind each policy costs?

Message 2

Across one insurance broker's team we found about 870 hours a year. The biggest items were filing emails, building document packs and pricing quotes. We find where the hours go and what they're costing, then build the fixes. I've put it on one page if you'd like to see it?

## Open 0-2 minutes
 * "Thanks for making the time. The plan for the next 20 minutes is to see where your team's hours go."
 * "I'll ask five questions. By the end I should know enough to write you one page you can take to whoever signs this kind of thing off."
 * One line on what we do: "We interview people role by role, in confidence, show leadership where the hours go and what they cost, then build the fixes on the systems you already run. The hours go back to your existing team."
 * Then, using their reply: "You replied to the note about [typing the same details into more than one system / the cost of the admin behind each policy]. What made you answer that one?" Let them talk; it often answers question 1.

## Five questions 2-15 minutes

## 1. Which team, and how many

"If we started with one team or function, which would it be? Roughly how many people are in it, and does it sit centrally or across the firms you've brought in?"

The page needs one scope: a named team and a rough headcount. In a group, the answer is often the back office of the most recent acquisitions, or a central operations or finance team.

Listen for: a named team, a number of people, and whether each acquired firm still runs its own version of the same work.

## 2. The time map (must ask)

"Walk me through that team's week. Which jobs come round again and again? For each one, how often does it happen, and roughly how long does it take each time?"

This becomes the table on the page: task, how often, how long, worked out together. Aim for 3 to 6 tasks. Prompts if they stall: typing the same details into more than one system, filing emails once they are dealt with, building document packs, pricing quotes, reports built by hand, chasing. Ask: "Does each firm you've bought do that differently?"

Listen for: specific, repeated tasks with a frequency and a time ("every renewal, about 20 minutes"). Say each one back and get them to confirm the numbers out loud. Write the whole-team total, never hours against one task.

## 3. What the time is worth (must ask)

"If your team got that time back, what would it go on, and what's that worth to you?"

Their words go on the page as the reason to go ahead. Write down what they say, not your own sum.

Listen for: growth they can't take on now, integrating the next acquisition without adding cost, faster quotes or renewals, finance closing sooner, or a figure in their own words.

## 4. Who signs, and who else (must ask)

"Who approves spend like this, and what's their title? Is there anyone else who should be involved, such as whoever leads integration, finance or IT?"

The page has to work for the person who signs. In a PE-backed group that may be the group CEO or CFO, or the board.

Listen for: a name and a title, whether they are on this call, and who else will read the page before a decision.

## 5. Timing

"What else is going on over the next few months? Another acquisition, firms moving onto one system, year end?"

Sets when interviews can happen and who is free to talk. Tie it to the trigger you checked before the call.

Listen for: a deal closing, a system move or a brand merger with a date, and a window when the team can spare time for interviews.

## Fits, and doesn't fit

## Signs it fits
 * A UK group or large independent of about 150 to 2,000 staff, with a UK decision-maker.
 * Buying firms and bringing their back offices together, with each firm still doing the same admin its own way.
 * A named owner of operations or integration who feels the hours.
 * Repeated work they can describe: rekeying between systems, filing, document packs, quotes, reports built by hand.
 * They want capacity for growth from the team they already have.

## Signs it doesn't
 * Decisions sit in a global programme or a parent with no UK decision-maker.
 * Well above 2,000 staff with no single UK division or regional business to start in.
 * A broker network or platform whose members are other brokers. Note it as a possible partner, not a buyer.
 * An insurtech or a firm building its own AI to sell.
 * Well below about 150 staff: a different route, not this proposal.
 * The goal they describe is cutting jobs.

## If they say

"What about confidentiality and our data?"

We interview in confidence; nothing identifies a person in what leadership sees. Findings are shared at team level.

"We have an IT team."

Good, we'd want them involved. We build on the systems you already run and work alongside them; the interviews show where the hours go, which helps them decide what to fix first.

"We're mid-integration, it's too busy right now."

Understood. Tell me when the team could spare time for interviews and I'll set the page around that date.

"Just send me something."

That's exactly what happens next: one page within two working days, built from what you've told me today, with what we'd look at, who we'd speak to and the cost.

"What does it cost?"

It depends on how many people we speak to; it'll be on the page.

"Is this about reducing headcount?"

The hours go back to the team you have, for growth and the work only they can do.

"We're regulated. How does AI fit with that?"

Where a judgement call is involved, a person approves it before anything goes out. We'd set that out for your team before anything is built.

## Close 15-20 minutes

Read back the time map and their words on what the time is worth, then:

I'll send you one page within two working days: what we'd look at, who we'd speak to, and the cost. Does that work?

If pushed on price: "It depends on how many people we speak to; it'll be on the page."

## Before you hang up, have you got
 * The team or function, and roughly how many people are in it (central, or across acquired firms).
 * 3 to 6 repeated tasks, each with how often and how long, confirmed out loud.
 * What the freed time would go on and what it's worth to them, in their own words.
 * Who approves the spend, with their title, and anyone else who should be involved.
 * Timing: the next acquisition, system move, brand merger or year end, and when interviews could happen.

## Never say
 * Any price, on the call or in a follow-up message. The cost goes on the page only.
 * The paid assessment, its price, any money-back guarantee, or "small business".
 * Any client's name, their systems, their team size or any pound figure from a client report.
 * "Marine" next to the 870 figure. It is "an insurance broker".
 * Hours against a single task. Figures are whole-team totals; tasks are examples ("across the team; the biggest items were...").
 * "Saved" for those figures. They are hours found, and none has been measured after a build.
 * Any figure, statistic or story beyond about 770, 870 or 780 hours a year.
 * That their data will stay in the UK.
 * "Headcount", "monitor", "track", or anything that sounds like watching people. Talk about the work and the systems.
 * Anything about their competitors or other firms in their market.
 * "Blueprint Two".
 * Any line that assumes they doubt us, and no "not this, but that" comparisons.

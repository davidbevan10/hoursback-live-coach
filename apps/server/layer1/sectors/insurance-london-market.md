London market call

## Insurers and London market: the 20-minute call

London market and specialty firms (wholesale, reinsurance and Lloyd's brokers, MGAs, managing agents) and specialist insurers or Lloyd's carriers, about 80-2,000 staff. On the call: an operations, finance or transformation leader, or a CEO, CFO or finance director.

0-2 min: open2-15 min: questions15-20 min: close

## Before the call 2 minutes
 * They came through the Grip LinkedIn campaign: a connection request with no note, then Message 1, then Message 2, then the booking link (cal.com/hoursback/enterprise-conversation). Check which message they replied to.
 * Check their campaign. INS-HOURS (COO, operations, transformation titles) replied to the question about typing the same details into more than one system. INS-MONEY (CEO, MD, CFO, finance director) replied to the question about what the admin behind each policy costs. Open on the one they answered.
 * Check the type of firm. A London market firm (broker, MGA, managing agent) means placement and bordereaux administration. A specialist insurer or Lloyd's carrier means the person should sit in operations, finance or transformation; the finance function is closest to work we have already done.
 * Look for a dated trigger in the last six months: a new COO or operations role, a restructure to remove silos, a new parent adding reporting, a stated plan to grow. If their recent news is about job cuts, keep it low key and stay on capacity.
 * Have a notepad ready for the time map: task, how often, how long, whose desk. That table goes straight onto the one-page proposal.
 * Sequences pause from 21 October to 4 November. If this call is close to that, make sure the one page lands within two working days, before the pause.

## What they were sent

Message 1, hours version (operations titles)

{first_name}, roughly how much of the week at {company} goes on typing the same details into more than one system?

Message 1, money version (CEO and finance titles)

{first_name}, does anyone at {company} have a figure for what the admin behind each policy costs?

Message 2

In one specialist insurer's finance team we found about 770 hours a year across the team. The biggest items were board packs and data reconciliation done by hand. We find where the hours go and what they're costing, then build the fixes. I've put it on one page if you'd like to see it?

## Open 0-2 minutes
 * "Thanks for making the time. This is 20 minutes to see where the hours go in your team."
 * Hours reply: "You came back on my question about typing the same details into more than one system. I'd like to start there."
 * Money reply: "You came back on my question about what the admin behind each policy costs. I'd like to start there, and the way we get to a cost is by mapping the time first."
 * "I'll ask five questions. By the end I want to know enough to put one page in front of you: what we'd look at, who we'd speak to, and the cost."
 * "Briefly, what we do: we interview people role by role, in confidence, show leadership where the hours go and what they cost, then build the fixes on the systems you already run. The hours go back to your existing team."

## Five questions 2-15 minutes

## 1. The team

"Which team would you want us to look at first, and roughly how many people are in it?"

The number of people sets who we interview and the cost on the page. One function is the right size to start.

Listen for: one named team with a rough headcount, such as placement support, delegated authority or bordereaux operations, finance, or credit control. If they name the whole firm, ask: "If we started with one team, which would it be?"

## 2. The time map (must ask)

"Walk me through that team's week. What do they do over and over? For each one, roughly how often, and how long does it take each time?"

This becomes the table on the proposal: task, how often, how long. Get three to six tasks. Say each one back so they confirm it.

Listen for: repeated work they can size, such as typing the same details into more than one system, bordereaux received and checked by hand, placement paperwork, reconciliations, month-end and board reporting built by hand, chasing for missing information. Say it back: "So that's about X hours a week across the team, and the biggest items are..."

## 3. What the time is worth (must ask)

"If your team got that time back, what would it go on, and what's that worth to you?"

Their words go onto the page as the reason to go ahead. Write down what they say, not your own sum.

Listen for: growth without adding people, faster month end or reporting, more time for brokers or underwriters to spend on clients, cleaner data for a new parent or board. A figure in pounds is a bonus; their own words are the requirement.

## 4. Who signs (must ask)

"Who approves spend like this, and what's their title? Is there anyone else who should be involved?"

The page has to work for whoever signs. In larger or group-owned firms that may be a CFO, a group COO or a parent company.

Listen for: a named person and title, whether it needs a board or a parent's sign-off, and anyone in finance, IT or compliance who would want a say.

## 5. Timing

"What else is going on over the next few months? An integration, year end, a system change, a restructure?"

The start date on the page has to fit around what is already happening.

Listen for: dates. Year end, a renewal season, a new system going live, a new parent's reporting starting. Ask when the team would have room for interviews.

## Fits, and doesn't fit

## Signs it fits
 * A UK decision-maker who can commission the work without a global programme.
 * They own operations, finance or transformation, or are the CEO, CFO or finance director.
 * They can name one team and its repeated work, and give rough times.
 * Placement, bordereaux, reconciliation or reporting work done by hand comes up quickly.
 * A recent trigger: a new COO, a restructure, a new parent adding reporting, a plan to grow.
 * They talk about capacity for growth with the team they have.

## Signs it doesn't
 * Decisions sit with a global or overseas programme and they cannot commission locally.
 * They sit in underwriting, sales or claims handling and do not own operations.
 * They want to cut headcount. We put hours back into the existing team.
 * The firm is a network or platform for other brokers, or builds its own AI as its product.
 * No repeated work they can describe, or the team is far below about 80 staff in the firm.
 * Anything health, NHS or GP related.

## If they say

"How do you handle confidentiality and our data?"

We interview people in confidence, and findings are shared at team level. How any build handles your data is agreed with your team before it starts.

"We have an IT team."

Good, we'd work with them. We build on the systems you already run, and the interviews give them a list of what to fix first with the hours against each item.

"We're mid-integration, or just too busy right now."

Tell me the dates and I'll put a start on the page that fits around them. The page itself takes nothing from your team.

"Just send me something."

Happy to. After this call I'll send one page within two working days, built from what you've told me, so it's about your team.

"What does it cost?"

It depends on how many people we speak to; it'll be on the page.

"Have you done this in a firm like ours?"

In one specialist insurer's finance team we found about 770 hours a year across the team; the biggest items were board packs and data reconciliation done by hand. Those are hours found, and the page will show what we'd look at in yours.

"We're regulated. Where does AI come in?"

We build workflows, integrations and AI agents, and a person approves anything that needs judgement. Which tools fit is something we agree with your team before any build.

## Close 15-20 minutes

"I'll send you one page within two working days: what we'd look at, who we'd speak to, and the cost. Does that work?"

If they push for a price: "It depends on how many people we speak to; it'll be on the page." Then confirm the email address to send it to.

## Before you hang up, have you got
 * The team or function, and roughly how many people are in it.
 * Three to six repeated tasks, each with how often and how long, said back and confirmed.
 * What freed time would go on and what it's worth to them, in their own words.
 * Who approves the spend, their title, and anyone else who should be involved.
 * Timing: integration, year end, a system change, or anything else going on, with dates.

## Never say
 * Any price on the call, the assessment price, any money-back guarantee, or "small business".
 * Any client's name, or anything that points to which insurer the 770 came from: its systems, its office location, its team size, or any other detail from that work.
 * That the fixes at that insurer have been built or measured. The 770 is hours found.
 * "X hours going on" one task. Hours are whole-team totals; tasks are examples of the biggest items.
 * "Saved". Say "found".
 * The broker figure or the word "marine" with London market firms. Use the 770 figure.
 * Anything about cutting headcount or replacing people, or "monitor", "track" and "wasted wages".
 * That their data will stay in the UK, or where any data is stored.
 * "Blueprint Two readiness". It was wound down.
 * Lines that set up a contrast to knock down, or that assume they doubt us.

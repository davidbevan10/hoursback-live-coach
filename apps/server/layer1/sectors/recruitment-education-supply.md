Education supply call

## Education supply: the 20-minute call

Education supply group (teachers, teaching assistants, SEND), often multi-brand or PE-backed, 150+ internal staff. On the call: an operations, compliance or back-office leader, or the CEO, MD or CFO.

0-2 min: open2-15 min: questions15-20 min: close

## Before the call 2 minutes
 * They replied on LinkedIn to the recruitment campaign. Check which Message 1 they got: operations, compliance and back-office titles got the hours version; CEO, MD and finance titles got the money version (the candidate database). That sets your angle.
 * Check the lead record for a trigger in the last six months: an acquisition, a new owner, a brand merge, or a new COO or operations director.
 * The pressure on this sector: academy trusts must buy supply staff through a government framework that caps agency fees, so margin per booking is under pressure. Let them raise it. Do not quote fee figures.
 * The chain that repeats for every booking: registration, right-to-work, vetting and safeguarding, the compliance file, timesheets, the payroll handoff, invoicing and client reporting. This is the skeleton for the time map.
 * Our recruitment figure is about 780 hours a year across one recruitment team, with the biggest items on the consultant desks. It was not an education supply back office, and nothing from it is built yet. Say both plainly if asked.
 * Money angle: the candidate database is what a buyer of the business pays for. If they are PE-backed or have bought firms, the state of that database matters to them.

## What they were sent

Message 1, hours version (operations titles)

{first_name}, how much of a consultant's day at {company} goes on admin rather than calls?

Message 1, money version (CEO and finance titles)

{first_name}, how confident are you in the data in {company}'s candidate database?

Message 2

In one recruitment team we found about 780 hours a year across the team. The biggest items were on the consultant desks, including logging calls into the CRM by hand. We find where the hours go and what they're costing, then build the fixes. I've put it on one page if you'd like to see it?

## Open 0-2 minutes
 * "Thanks for making the time. This is 20 minutes to see where the hours go in your team."
 * "I'll ask about five things. By the end I should know enough to send you one page you can take to whoever signs."
 * Hours angle: "You replied about admin on the desks, so let's start with where the week goes."
 * Money angle: "You replied about the candidate database, so let's start with how it gets kept up to date, and who does that."
 * One line on us if asked: "We interview people role by role, in confidence, show you where the hours go and what they cost, then build the fixes on the systems you already run. The hours go back to your existing team."

## Five questions 2-15 minutes

## 1. The team

"Which team should we be looking at: the central back office, compliance, or the consultant desks? Roughly how many people are in it? And do the brands share one back office, or does each run its own?"

This tells us who we would interview and how many people, which drives the cost on the page.

Listen for: a named function with a headcount, and one back office handling bookings for several brands.

## 2. The time map (must ask)

"Let's walk one booking through, from registering a teacher to invoicing the school. Which steps repeat most, how often do they happen, and roughly how long does each one take?"

This becomes the table on the page: task, how often, how long. Get 3-6 tasks. Name the bottleneck, never a tool. Say each one back: "So that's about [X] minutes, [Y] times a week, across the team?" and get a yes. Money angle: also ask "Where does the candidate database get updated by hand, or not at all?"

Listen for: the same details typed into more than one system; compliance files checked by hand; timesheets chased; reports for schools built by hand; database records that go stale.

## 3. What it's worth (must ask)

"If your team got that time back, what would it go on, and what's that worth to you?"

Their own words go on the page. Write what they say, not your sum.

Listen for: more bookings per person, protecting margin per booking, consultants back on calls, a candidate database the business can rely on and that holds its value.

## 4. Who signs (must ask)

"Who would approve spend like this, and what's their title? Is there anyone else who should be involved, say compliance, finance or IT?"

The page has to work for the person who signs, and they may not be on this call.

Listen for: a named title. In PE-backed or multi-brand groups, ask whether it sits with the group or the brand.

## 5. Timing

"What else is going on in the next few months: an acquisition to bring in, brands being merged, a system change, year end?"

Sets when the work could start and what to avoid. It goes in "how to go ahead" on the page.

Listen for: a deal or system change in progress (it affects when interviews can happen), or a clear quiet window.

## Fits, and doesn't fit

## Signs it fits
 * 150+ internal (office) staff, with a central back office
 * Several brands, a recent acquisition or a new owner
 * They can walk the booking chain and put times on it
 * They talk about margin per booking or trust in the database
 * A named person owns the back office and can sign or open the door

## Signs it doesn't
 * Under 100 internal staff once temps on the books are taken out
 * Health or social care staffing (nursing, care, locums)
 * A single franchisee rather than the central team
 * Decisions all made by a parent outside the UK
 * They want fewer people rather than time back for the team
 * They build their own AI or automation to sell

## If they say

"Our candidate and vetting data is sensitive."

We interview in confidence; nothing identifies a person in what leadership sees, and findings are shared at team level. The interviews are about how the work gets done, so we do not need to go through candidate files to map the hours.

"We have an IT team."

Good, we'd want them involved. We find where the hours go first, then build on the systems you already run, alongside them.

"We're mid-integration" or "We're too busy right now."

The one page will say how long it takes and who we'd need to speak to, so you can choose the timing.

"Just send me something."

I will: one page after this call, built from what you've told me. That's why I'm asking about the week.

"What does it cost?"

It depends on how many people we speak to; it'll be on the page.

"Have you done this for an education supply firm?"

Not in education supply yet. The closest is a recruitment team where we found about 780 hours a year across the team; the biggest items were on the consultant desks.

"We already have a booking and compliance system."

We build on what you already run, so the system stays. We look at the work around it: the rekeying, checking and chasing.

## Close 15-20 minutes

"I'll send you one page within two working days: what we'd look at, who we'd speak to, and the cost. Does that work?"

## Before you hang up, have you got
 * The team or function, roughly how many people, and whether brands share a back office
 * 3-6 repeated tasks, each with how often and how long, said back and confirmed
 * What freed time would go on and what it's worth to them, in their words
 * Who approves spend like this (name and title), plus anyone else to involve
 * Timing: acquisitions, brand merges, system changes, year end

## Never say
 * Any price or day rate on the call, or any guarantee
 * "Small business"
 * Any client's name, their systems, or their team size
 * "X hours going on [task]". Hours are whole-team totals; tasks are examples ("across the team; the biggest items were...")
 * "Saved". Hours are "found"
 * That we have built anything for a recruitment firm
 * "Your data stays in the UK", or anything about where data is stored
 * Fee cap figures, or detail on safeguarding guidance changes
 * Anything about fewer staff or headcount, or "monitor", "track", "most efficient employee", "wasted wages"
 * "Not X but Y" contrasts, or lines that assume they doubt us
 * Any school, trust or education client by name
 * If insurance comes up: 870 hours is "an insurance broker", never marine

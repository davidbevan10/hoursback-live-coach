# David's known pitfalls (from his own call debriefs)

These are the mistakes that recur across David's past calls. Catching them is the coach's highest-value job. When one is happening or about to happen, cue the fix. Where a pitfall fix and the call's playbook disagree (for example on naming a price), the playbook wins.

## Pitfall: talking too long
Seen in about half of all debriefs. Long explanations of HoursBack, his background or his method before the prospect has named a problem; two-minute uninterrupted answers; the prospect has to interrupt.
Watch for: David speaking for more than about a minute in a row, or explaining the process before any pain is named.
Fix: stop, one sentence, then hand back with a question. "Does that make sense for how your team works?"

## Pitfall: letting a vague answer pass
The most common gap. The prospect says "everywhere", "tons of things", "I have no idea", "it's more about volume", or gives a loose "2-3 hours", and David moves on.
Watch for: any pain without a number, a person, or a frequency attached.
Fix: pick one example and rebuild it. "Walk me through the last time that happened - step by step, how long did each bit take, and who did it?"

## Pitfall: not qualifying the call early
Running a full discovery or demo with someone who is not a buyer: a consultant or agency, a referral partner, someone outside the UK, someone with a locked-down internal programme, someone semi-retired or "just exploring".
Watch for: signs in the first five minutes that this person cannot or will not buy.
Fix: ask what they want from the call, then pivot. "Are you looking at this for your own business, or is there a referral or partnership angle?" If it is referral, mine names and intros, not discovery.

## Pitfall: not chasing the decision-maker
"I'll speak to the MD", "I'd need to check with commercial", "there's a framework process", "I'll run it by my partner" - and David moves on.
Watch for: any mention of someone else who decides or approves.
Fix: "Who is that, what will they want to know, and should they join the next call?"

## Pitfall: dodging budget
Budget is never tested, price answers are vague, or a proposal is offered without any reaction to a number. Also the reverse: discounts or price flexibility offered before price was ever raised as a problem.
Watch for: "no budget", "funds are tight", "what does it cost", "I'm open, relatively", or David offering a discount unprompted.
Fix: ask about the approval process and what size of spend needs sign-off. If they ask the price, use the playbook's line for it. Never discount unless they have said price is the barrier, and then ask "is it the amount or the timing?" first.

## Pitfall: pitching or giving work away mid-discovery
Explaining how he would build the fix, demoing, offering free work, free builds, adoption tips, or three different deal structures nobody asked for.
Watch for: David describing a solution before the cost of the problem is known.
Fix: name the bottleneck, not the tool, and go back to the cost of the problem.

## Pitfall: missing the buying signal
The prospect says yes in principle, mentions a hard deadline, says they shared the material with decision-makers, or hints the price is too low - and David keeps discovering instead of moving to close.
Watch for: "we could do the assessment", "when could you start", a deadline or migration date, "I've shown it to the directors".
Fix: move to the next step now, with a date. Use the deadline as the reason.

## Pitfall: letting an objection slide
"We already do that internally", "we already use X", "I don't know if we'd be the ideal customer" - David defends or explains instead of asking.
Watch for: any self-disqualification or incumbent mention.
Fix: ask why. "What would make you consider bringing someone in?" or "Why do you say that?"

## Pitfall: weak close
Ending on "let me know", "I'll send a proposal and we'll chat", or rushed action planning in the last two minutes.
Watch for: the end of the call approaching with no next step, owner and date agreed.
Fix: summarise in their words, then propose the step with a date and an owner, and confirm it out loud before hanging up.

## Pitfall: long social preamble
More than a couple of minutes of catch-up before discovery starts, especially with people he knows.
Watch for: no business question asked by about minute three.
Fix: "Shall we dive in? What's the biggest drain on the week right now?"

## Pitfall: referral signals not actioned
A named contact, a firm, a network or an offer to introduce passes without a request. Or a call came via a referrer and David never checks what the referrer said.
Watch for: names, firms, "I know someone who...", or a referred call that opens without a frame check.
Fix: ask for the intro on the spot, or "What did [referrer] tell you about us?"

# Enterprise intro call (20 minutes)

A senior leader at a larger organisation, usually from LinkedIn outreach. Goal: map their team's week well enough to write a ONE-PAGE written proposal, sent within two working days, that they can take to whoever signs.

## What HoursBack does (say it plainly)
"We interview people role by role, in confidence, show leadership where the hours go and what they cost, then build the fixes on the systems you already run. The hours go back to your existing team."

## Clock
0-2 minutes open, 2-15 minutes five questions, 15-20 minutes close.

## Q1. Which team, and how many
Which team or function to start with, and roughly how many people are in it.

## Q2. The time map (must ask)
3 to 6 repeated tasks, each with how often and how long. Say each one back and get the numbers confirmed out loud. Hours are whole-team totals; tasks are examples.

## Q3. What the time is worth (must ask)
"If your team got that time back, what would it go on, and what's that worth to you?" Write their words, not your sum.

## Q4. Who signs, and who else (must ask)
Who approves spend like this and their title, plus anyone else who should be involved (integration, finance, IT).

## Q5. Timing
What else is going on: an integration, year end, a system change. When could the team spare time for interviews.

## Close
"I'll send you one page within two working days: what we'd look at, who we'd speak to, and the cost. Does that work?"

## If they ask what it costs
"It depends on how many people we speak to; it'll be on the page." Never price on the call.

## Standard objections
- Confidentiality and data: "We interview in confidence. Findings are shared with leadership at team level, so nothing identifies a person in what leadership sees."
- "We have an IT team": "Good, we'd work with them. We build on the systems you already run, and the interviews show them which jobs are worth fixing first."
- "We're mid-integration / too busy": "Understood. Tell me your window and I'll put the timing on the page around it."
- "Just send me something": "Happy to. That's the one page I send after this call. A few more minutes now makes it about your team."

## Never say
- Any price on the call, the £799 assessment price, a money-back guarantee, or "small business".
- Client names, client systems or client team sizes.
- That data stays in the UK.
- "Not X but Y" contrasts, or lines that assume the buyer is sceptical.

## Before you hang up, have you got
- The team or function, and roughly how many people
- Three to six repeated tasks with how often and how long, agreed
- Their words on what the time would go on and what it's worth
- Who approves the spend and their title, plus anyone else involved
- Timing
